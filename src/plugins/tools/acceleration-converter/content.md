## Acceleration as change in velocity

Acceleration describes how quickly velocity changes. The converter uses meters per second squared as its reference and supports feet per second squared, Galileo (`1 Gal = 0.01 m/s²`) and standard gravity (`1 g = 9.80665 m/s²`).

For a vehicle test reporting `0.5 g`, the result is about `4.903325 m/s²`. That conversion is useful when comparing an accelerometer specification with an engineering calculation. The standard-gravity constant is a defined reference; it is not a measurement of local gravity at every location.

Acceleration is not speed. A car traveling at a steady 100 km/h has zero acceleration in a straight line, while a stationary sensor can report gravitational acceleration. This tool converts scalar magnitudes and does not resolve direction, vectors or changing acceleration over time.

Use [Speed Converter](../../speed-converter/) for velocity units or [Force Converter](../../force-converter/) when acceleration is part of a mass–force calculation.
