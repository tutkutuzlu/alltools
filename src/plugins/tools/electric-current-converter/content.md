## Scale current across SI prefixes

Electric current is the rate of charge flow. The ampere is the reference; microamperes, milliamperes and kiloamperes differ by decimal powers of ten. Thus `250 mA` equals `0.25 A` or `250,000 µA`.

This is useful when a sensor datasheet specifies microamps, a circuit supply is labeled in milliamps and a calculation expects amperes. Prefix conversion is exact for the unit definitions, subject only to numeric display formatting.

Changing units does not determine whether a conductor or component can safely carry the current. Current alone also does not reveal voltage, resistance, energy or power; those relationships require circuit conditions such as Ohm's law. AC and DC values can have different meanings—instantaneous, peak or RMS—but the tool converts the entered magnitude without interpreting it.

Use [Voltage Converter](../../voltage-converter/) for voltage prefixes and [Power Converter](../../power-converter/) only when you already have a power quantity rather than trying to infer it from current alone.
